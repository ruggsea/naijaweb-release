# Codebook — Hausa, Igbo and Yorùbá web text

You are shown one document at a time. It has already been tagged by a machine
as being in **your language**. Two questions, one keypress:

**Is the language right, and is the text any good?**

---

## The four keys

**`1` — Right language, good quality**
It is your language, and it reads like real writing by a person. Keep it.

**`2` — Right language, bad quality**
It is your language, but the text is bad. Bad means: very wrongly spelled
text, or just boilerplate (menus, navigation, cookie notices, tags,
"click here"), or one sentence repeated over and over, or a single stray
sentence with nothing around it.

**`3` — Wrong language**
It is real writing, but not your language. Another language, or mostly
English with a few words of yours.

**`4` — Not language**
Gibberish. Not understandable as any language at all. Random characters,
code, numbers, broken encoding, word salad.

---

## How to decide — the overall vibe

**Judge the document as a whole, not sentence by sentence.**

- **A few misspellings are fine.** That is still `1`. Almost nothing on the
  web is perfectly spelled.
- **Missing diacritics are fine.** Text written without tone marks or dots
  below is still your language and still `1` if it otherwise reads naturally.
  This is normal on the web, not an error.
- **The test for `1`:** if the text is *somewhat intelligible*, looks like
  *natural language*, and the language is the right one — keep it. Be
  forgiving. When most of the document is in the correct language, that is
  enough.
- Only reach for `2` when the text is genuinely unusable as writing.
- If it is mostly your language but has an English headline or a stray
  English paragraph, that is still `1` or `2` — not `3`.

**When you are torn between two keys, pick the more generous one.** We would
rather keep a borderline document and sort it out later than throw away real
text in a language that has very little of it.

---

## Notes

Some documents are shown to more than one person on purpose, so we can
measure how much we agree. Do not discuss documents with each other.

There is no time pressure, but most documents can be judged in a few seconds
from the first paragraph. You do not need to read to the end.

---

*The four categories follow the error taxonomy of Kreutzer et al. (2022),
"Quality at a Glance: An Audit of Web-Crawled Multilingual Datasets", TACL —
correct (C), correct but boilerplate or short (CB/CS), wrong language (WL),
and non-linguistic content (NL) — collapsed to four keys, with their
instruction to raters to be forgiving when most of the text is in the correct
language.*
